# TikTok Landing Page Checklist: Match the Promise in Your Video

A TikTok landing page should let a viewer recognize the product they just saw. If the video demonstrates one worksheet and the page opens with an unrelated bundle, the visitor has to reconstruct the connection before they can assess the offer.

Message match means the page describes the product and task your video showed, with the requirements and evidence to support its promise. Check that connection before directing viewers to the page.

This checklist is for a solo creator selling one digital product. It belongs within the broader [TikTok product and delivery workflow](https://groniz.com/blog/how-to-make-money-by-automating-your-tiktok/), after the video is drafted and before its CTA is approved.

## Put the video and page beside each other

Write down four things from the video: its opening promise, the product shown, the result demonstrated, and the action requested at the end. Use the actual words and frames rather than a summary of what you intended.

Then open the destination through the route available to your viewers. Read it without your seller login. Compare the page against those four items, beginning with what appears before the visitor has to search for more information.

You can use different wording on the page as long as a visitor can recognize the offer from the video. A broader product description can appear later, once the task that brought the visitor there is recognizable.

If the closing instruction is still vague, work through the [TikTok CTA examples](https://groniz.com/blog/tiktok-call-to-action-examples-for-an-owned-product) first. You need a specific closing instruction to check against the page.

## A hypothetical mismatch to repair

Imagine a creator selling a downloadable freelance project workbook. Their video shows a sample project brief becoming a checklist of deliverables. The hypothetical CTA directs viewers to a tested product-page route to inspect the worksheet. All examples below are hypothetical, not measured conversion results.

The destination's headline says "Take control of your freelance business." Its first screenshot shows a weekly calendar. The deliverables worksheet appears farther down, and the product's software requirement is difficult to locate.

The creator can repair this without making a new promise. They bring the deliverables worksheet into the first relevant example, describe the task demonstrated in the video, and place the software requirement near the offer details. They label the input shown in the screenshot as sample data.

They also discover that the page calls the download a "complete client-management system." The demonstrated workbook does not support that scope. They replace the broad claim with a concrete description of the included planning worksheets.

Those edits make it easier to connect the video to the worksheet for sale. To find out whether purchases changed, the creator would need evidence from their own systems.

## Copy the promise-audit worksheet

For each row, record pass, fix, or hold. Pass means you checked it and the page matches. Fix means you have identified a specific correction. Hold means the unresolved issue should stop this video from directing viewers to the page.

| Audit item | Compare this evidence | Pass condition | Example reason to fix or hold |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Opening task | Video opening and page introduction | The same buyer task is recognizable | Page leads with a different use case |
| Product identity | Recorded interface and offer name | Visitor can identify the demonstrated download | Screenshot belongs to an unavailable version |
| Observable result | Demo output and page claim | Claim stays within what the evidence shows | Page promises a broader business outcome |
| Included files | Video references and contents list | Every implied inclusion is accurate | A shown worksheet costs extra but is presented as included |
| Requirements | Demo setup and page requirements | Necessary software and buyer inputs are clear | The requirement is missing or unverified |
| CTA destination | Closing line and first available action | Visitor can perform the promised next step | "See a sample" leads only to checkout |
| Purchase handoff | Offer details and order path | The correct product reaches the purchase step | Wrong product or broken route |
| Delivery expectation | Product description and download instructions | Buyer can understand what they receive after purchase | Page implies a service but sells a file |

Add the proposed correction, its owner, and a recheck date next to every fix or hold. If you handle every task yourself, write down the correction precisely enough that you can pick it up next time you open the file.

## Resolve holds before polishing copy

A broken purchase route deserves attention before a more appealing headline. A missing requirement can change whether the product suits the buyer at all. Fix issues that could prevent a purchase or mislead a buyer before spending time on wording and presentation.

For the hypothetical workbook, the broad client-management claim is a hold because it changes the product's apparent scope. A slightly different label for the same worksheet may be a fix if the visitor can still identify it. Decide whether to hold the video by considering what the mismatch could cause a buyer to misunderstand.

Recheck the complete route after the correction. It is possible to update the page while leaving an old screenshot or closing line in the video. Keep the video version and destination together in your approval notes.
